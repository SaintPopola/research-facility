# Omnisphere 3 Benchmark — the bar we're calibrating against

> **2026-06-03.** Compiled from Sound on Sound, MusicTech, MusicRadar, and Synth Anatomy reviews. Numbers vary slightly between sources; ranges shown. Use this as the *competitive context*, not as a clone target.

## New synthesis features in v3 (vs 2.8)

Verified across reviews:

- **Quadzone** — splits, crossfades, velocity switches across the 4 layers; MIDI fader sweep between them; per-layer envelope + aftertouch mapping
- **Polyphonic dual frequency shifter** (marketed as "world's first")
- **Circuit-modelled filter saturation** added to analog engine
- **New portamento glide module** with classic curves: OBXA, Moog, Odyssey, CS80 glissando
- **Oscillator drift function** for vintage analog instability simulation
- **Unison phase scatter** capability
- **Sweepable wavetables** added to wavetable engine
- **Full MPE support** (was partial in v2)
- **Patch mutation** — instant musically-related variations of a loaded patch
- **Global Controls** — adaptive macros for tone / ambience / filter / envelope / vibrato / unison

## New FX in v3

35 new effects modules added to the existing rack (now ~93-103 total depending on what counts):
- New reverbs: **Solar Shimmer**, **Inversions**
- New delays: **magnetic echo**
- Compressors, EQs, distortions across all categories
- **Standalone Omni FX plug-in** — the FX rack as an independent plug-in usable in any DAW (positions against Soundtoys)
- Up to **4 effects per FX rack**, **53 racks simultaneously** in a patch

## New library content in v3

**18 new patch libraries** with thematic names: Analog Vibes, Retro Vibes, Classic Digital, Scoring Electronic, Hard Edges, Warm Tones, Ambient Dreams, Club Land, Electronic Production, Electronic Underground, Scoring Organic, Organic Vibes, Experimental Organic, Instruments Collection, Live Keyboardist, Vocal Collection, SFX Electronics, SFX Organic.

**Deep-sampled new sources** specifically called out: ostrich egg, nyckelharpa, lyre harp, celestaphone, metallophones, EDM wavetables, vocal phrases.

## Browser overhaul

This is the v3 change most directly relevant to Research Facility's differentiator:

- **Redesigned "super-logical browser"** with themed sub-categories and consistent descriptors
- **Multi-axis filtering**: keyword, mood, timbre, instrument type, genre, atmosphere
- **Patch marking and rating** built in
- **Directory tree interface** alongside the search-driven view
- **Global filtration** across category columns

What's notable: Omnisphere itself recognized in v3 that *the browser is the differentiator* — they invested heavily in it. This validates our D5 strategy. Our edge is taking it further with AI semantic search.

## Performance (per reviews)

- Runs efficiently on consumer hardware (one reviewer tested on M2 MacBook Air)
- Scales properly to 13-inch displays (Omnisphere historically struggled at smaller sizes)
- Same disk footprint as v2 thanks to lossless audio compression improvements

## What we explicitly are NOT trying to match

- **41,405 sounds.** Solo dev cannot match. Reframe: smaller library, better organization + search.
- **300+ hardware integration profiles.** Multi-decade investment; not achievable.
- **53 FX racks simultaneously.** Vastly more than musicians actually use; over-engineering for our user.
- **638 morphing wavetables.** A curated 100-150 is plenty. Quality beats quantity.

## Strategic takeaways

1. **The browser is the explicit v3 differentiation.** Even Spectrasonics agrees the browser matters as much as the engine. Our AI search bet is well-positioned.
2. **Quality of curation > quantity.** 18 themed libraries with strong names is the marketing story they tell. We tell the same story with 6-8 themed packs that are tightly curated.
3. **The Quadzone idea** (4-layer splits + crossfades + velocity switches + MIDI fader) is sophisticated *but optional*. Our v1 ships a single layer with a great engine; Quadzone is v2.
4. **Standalone FX rack** is a clever side-product. Worth keeping in mind for v2 — gives a second SKU off the same codebase.
